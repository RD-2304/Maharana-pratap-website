Biography of Maharana Pratap

Early Life

Maharana Pratap was born on May 9, 1540, in Kumbhalgarh, Rajasthan. He was the eldest son of Maharana Udai Singh II and Rani Jaiwanti Bai. From a young age, he showed signs of bravery and leadership. He belonged to the royal family of Mewar and was part of the Sisodia Rajput dynasty.

His younger brothers were Shakti Singh, Vikram Singh, and Jagmal Singh. He had two stepsisters: Chand Kanwar and Man Kanwar. His chief consort was Ajabde Bai Punwar of Bijolia, and they had 22 sons and 5 daughters, including Amar Singh I.

Ascension to Throne

After his father's death in 1572, Rani Dheer Bai Bhatiyani wanted her son Jagmal to succeed him, but senior courtiers preferred Pratap as the eldest son. The nobles' desire prevailed, and Pratap ascended the throne as Maharana Pratap, the 54th ruler of Mewar in the line of the Sisodia Rajputs. He was crowned in Gogunda on the auspicious day of Holi.

Jagmal swore revenge and left for Ajmer to join the armies of Emperor Akbar, who later gave him a portion of Sirohi.

Resistance Against Mughals

Pratap refused to submit to Mughal emperor Akbar, leading to a long conflict. Akbar initially favored diplomacy but sent emissaries like Jalal Khan, Man Singh, Raja Bhagwant Das, and Todar Mal, all of whom failed. Pratap waged guerrilla warfare against the Mughals, never surrendering his independence. His capital was Chittorgarh, and later he established a new capital in the Aravalli hills at Chavand.

During his reign, Pratap's court at Chavand provided refuge to numerous poets, artists, writers, and artisans. The Chavand school of art emerged, supported by figures like Nasiruddin.

Reconquest of Mewar

Mughal pressure relaxed after 1579 due to rebellions elsewhere. Taking advantage, Pratap recovered Mughal-occupied areas, capturing 36 outposts. Udaipur, Gogunda, Mandal, and Pandwara were recaptured. By 1585, he had recovered a large part of Mewar, and citizens returned. Trade and agriculture revived.

Death and Legacy

Maharana Pratap reportedly died from injuries sustained in a hunting accident at Chavand on January 19, 1597, at the age of 56. He was succeeded by his eldest son, Amar Singh I. On his deathbed, he instructed his son never to submit to the Mughals and to reclaim Chittor.

Pratap is remembered as a symbol of resistance and patriotism. His defiance inspired later figures like Shivaji and anti-British revolutionaries. A statue of him was unveiled in the Parliament of India in 2007.
